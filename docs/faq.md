## About the Notes

### What are the Intelligence Field Notes?
Personal notes on intelligence analysis, OSINT method and report writing, made public for convenience. They collect in one place the method that the SOsintOps tools had each been carrying in their own copy of the guidelines, so that a correction made once reaches all of them.

### Are the notes complete or authoritative?
No. They are a practitioner's notes, with no claim to be exhaustive and no official standing. Where they summarise a standard (ICD 203, the UNODC manual, the Berkeley Protocol), the standard itself is linked and prevails. Where they give an opinion, it is an opinion.

### Who are the notes for?
Anyone who runs open-source investigations and has to turn them into something another person can rely on: analysts, investigators, journalists, researchers, students, and the users of the SOsintOps tools.

### Where should I start?
Read [Report Writing](report-writing.md) first: it shows where everything else is going. Then [Source Evaluation](source-evaluation.md) and [Estimative Language](estimative-language.md), which are short and change every report you write. Then [Analytic Techniques](analytic-techniques.md) with Heuer's [*Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/books-monographs/psychology-of-intelligence-analysis-2/) beside it. The [Reading List](reading-list.md) has the rest.

## Method Choices

### Why follow a US directive (ICD 203) outside government?
Because it is short, public, free and testable. Its five analytic and nine tradecraft standards describe what any careful analysis should show, and a reviewer can check a report against them line by line. The notes adapt it where government vocabulary does not fit (a "customer" may be a client, an editor or a court).

### Why the UNODC 4 x 4 system and not the Admiralty 6 x 6?
The 4 x 4 system grades information on how close the source was to it (personal knowledge, corroboration), which makes grading less subjective; the 6 x 6 system grades how plausible it sounds. The 4 x 4 is also the system of Europol and of the UNODC manual that the link-analysis method follows. One system everywhere means a grade written in one tool means the same in another. [Source Evaluation](source-evaluation.md#converting-from-the-6-x-6-admiralty-system) explains how to convert old 6 x 6 grades.

### Why only one row of the likelihood scale?
ICD 203 offers two equivalent rows and strongly discourages mixing them. A list that takes "remote" from one row and "very likely" from the other is the most common way it goes wrong. The notes and the SOsintOps templates use the first row: almost no chance, very unlikely, unlikely, roughly even chance, likely, very likely, almost certain.

## The Notes and the Tools

### How do the SOsintOps tools use these notes?
Each tool keeps its own short guidelines page about what is specific to its work (Wukong for Chinese sources, Quaesitor for link analysis, Argos and Speculator for their case folders and templates) and links here for the common method. The workstations that are used offline also carry a copy of these pages in a `field-notes` folder, generated by [`tools/sync_core.py`](../tools/sync_core.py) when the tool is released.

### Can I edit the copy of a page inside a tool's repository?
No: each copy starts with a line saying where it comes from, and the next synchronisation overwrites it. Correct the page here and run the synchronisation.

### Are there related SOsintOps projects?
Yes: workstations ([Argos](https://github.com/SOsintOps/Argos), [Speculator](https://github.com/SOsintOps/Speculator-Project), [Wukong](https://github.com/SOsintOps/Wukong)), a browser toolkit ([Exploratores](https://github.com/SOsintOps/Exploratores)), a case graph ([Quaesitor](https://github.com/SOsintOps/quaesitor)), a ransomware CTI aggregator ([Elementary CTI](https://github.com/SOsintOps/elementary-CTI)), a corporate-register analyser ([Aethelburg](https://github.com/SOsintOps/Aethelburg-project)) and more. See [Related Projects](related-projects.md).

## Licence and Contributions

### Can I reuse the notes?
Yes. The text is released under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](../LICENSE): copy, adapt and share it, also commercially, with credit and a link. The scripts in `tools/` are under the [MIT licence](../tools/LICENSE). The texts summarised here keep their own terms: the notes paraphrase them and quote only short passages.

### How can I suggest a correction?
Open an issue or a pull request on [GitHub](https://github.com/SOsintOps/intelligence-field-notes). A source for the correction is the fastest way to have it accepted.
